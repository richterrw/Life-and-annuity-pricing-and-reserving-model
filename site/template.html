Life & Annuity Pricing and Reserving Model

A working pricing and valuation model for two US individual products, built on official SOA mortality tables: a 20-year level term policy and a single-premium deferred income annuity. Both are priced to a 10% hurdle IRR, projected through statutory profit, tax and capital, and reserved with formula methods (CRVM, CARVM) and a stochastic reserve (CTE70) over 1,000 interest-rate scenarios.

What the model shows

The results most worth discussing, each backed by an output in the repository.

Term life is a mortality and expense business

Mortality 10% higher costs @@term_irr_mort_bp_abs of IRR; 10% more expense costs @@term_exp_bp_abs. The valuation rate barely matters (±3bp) because term reserves are small.

The annuity is a spread business

A 100bp fall in the earned rate wipes out @@dia_rate_down_abs of premium in PV of profit, roughly ten times any mortality shock. Restoring the hurdle means cutting the payout rate by @@dia_reprice_bp_abs.

Longevity risk lives in the improvement scale

Dropping Scale G2 adds @@dia_noimp of margin, while 150% of G2 costs @@dia_g2x15. A flat 10% cut in base mortality costs only @@dia_mort90.

Formula reserves are conservative for term, not for the annuity

The term stochastic reserve sits far below CRVM, the redundancy that principles-based reserving released. For the annuity, reinvestment risk lifts it above CARVM (@@dia_sr_pct vs. @@dia_carvm_pct).

Parameter risk does not diversify

Process risk falls as 1/√N: the 99.5% VaR goes from @@var1k_proc to @@var100k_proc of PV premium between 1,000 and 100,000 policies. A 10% uncertainty in the mortality level keeps it at @@var100k_param.

IRR is not always defined

Under some annuity shocks later earnings turn negative and the cash flows change sign several times, so the IRR is not unique. The model reports an IRR only when it is unique and judges the annuity on PV of profit.

How it works

Annual, curtate framework in ALTAM notation. The methodology document has every formula.

Mortality

SOA XTbML files are parsed directly. The 2017 CSO uses its 25-year select period; the 2012 IAR is projected generationally with Scale G2.

q_x(y) = q_x(2012) · (1 − G2_x)^(y − 2012)

CRVM reserve

First-year net premium α = cx, renewal β, with the expense allowance capped at the 19-pay-life premium per the Standard Valuation Law.

tV = A¹(x+t : n−t) − β · ä(x+t : n−t)

Profit test

Statutory gain after tax, less the increase in required capital, gives distributable earnings. The price solves PV at the hurdle rate equal to zero.

DE_t = (1−τ)·Gain_t + (1−τ)·i·RC_(t−1) − ΔRC_t

Stochastic reserve

For each scenario, the greatest present value of accumulated deficiencies: the starting assets that keep the balance non-negative. The reserve is its CTE70.

SR = CTE70( max_k Σ_(j≤k) PV_s(net outflow_j) )

What this demonstrates

The work an entry-level pricing or valuation actuary does, built from first principles.

Pricing

  • Gross premium and payout rate solved to a hurdle IRR
  • Distributable earnings with tax and required capital
  • Rate cards and premium build-ups
  • Repricing under stress

Valuation

  • NLP, CRVM (20-pay-life cap) and deficiency reserves
  • CARVM for a deferred income annuity
  • VM-20 / VM-22 stochastic reserve mechanics
  • Valuation vs. pricing bases kept separate

Risk & engineering

  • Interest scenario generator, CTE and VaR
  • Monte Carlo mortality: process vs. parameter risk
  • Python, NumPy, pandas; tested, linted, CI
  • Formatted Excel and reproducible notebook
